There's a genre with no shelf label. It isn't horror. Nothing jumps out. But you finish the last page and something stays in the room with you for a day or two, standing just behind your left shoulder, being quiet.

We call these *slightly haunted* books, and the studio has strong, unqualified opinions about them.

The common thread is loss you can't quite locate: a house that's changed, a person you can only reach by remembering, a country that stopped existing on schedule. The prose is usually calm. That's what does it. Nobody in these books is screaming. They're setting the table, describing the weather, mentioning in passing that someone is gone.

The Extremely Casual Book Club, which does not meet and has no reading list, unofficially reads only these. It's easy to keep up. You read one, you sit with it for a week, you tell no one, you slowly become a slightly more melancholic person at parties.

If you want a starting point: anything where the narrator is remembering a summer. That's it. That's the whole recommendation. Remembered summers are the most haunted season we have.

We don't sell books. We do sell a pin for the club, which is the same as attending. Wear it to the meeting that isn't happening.
